# Paired conjunctions

**Paired (correlative) conjunctions** come in fixed pairs. Each first word must be followed by its own partner, and the two parts usually join items of the same grammatical form.

| First part | Second part | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| both | and | the two together |
| either | or | one of the two |
| neither | nor | not one and not the other |
| not only | but also | the first and, in addition, the second |
| whether | or | two possibilities (often after verbs like "know," "determine," "study") |

**1. Do not mix the pairs.** These are always wrong:
- both ... or, both ... as well as
- either ... nor, either ... and
- neither ... or
- not only ... and also
- whether ... nor

**2. Keep the parts parallel** when you can: "both **store** water **and lose** very little," "either **by river** or **by wagon**." TOEFL usually tests the partner word, not small differences in parallel form.

**3. Verb agreement with "or" and "nor"**: when two subjects are joined by "either ... or" or "neither ... nor," the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: "Neither the Moon nor Mercury **has** a thick atmosphere." "Neither the teacher nor the students **were** ready."

**4. "Not only" at the start of a sentence** causes inversion: "Not only **did the Wright brothers build** airplanes, but they also tested them carefully." (See P12.)

## Examples

- Platypuses **both** lay eggs **and** produce milk.
- The kiwi can **neither** fly **nor** see well.
- The Wright brothers were **not only** skilled mechanics **but also** careful observers.

## Strategy

- When you see "both," "either," "neither," "not only," or "whether," immediately look ahead for the partner word.
- If the partner word is underlined, check that it is the correct one.
- If the first word is underlined, look back from the partner: "... and" needs "both"; "... or" needs "either" or "whether."

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **"Both ... or"**: "can both store water or lose very little" should be "and".
- **"Neither ... or"**: "can neither fly or see well" should be "nor".
- **"Not only ... and also"**: should be "not only ... but also".
- **"Either ... and"**: "either expensive and unreliable" should be "both expensive and unreliable".
- **"Whether ... nor"**: should be "whether ... or".
