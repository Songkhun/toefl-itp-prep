# Verb tenses and time expressions

On the TOEFL, the correct tense is usually decided by a **time expression** somewhere in the sentence. Find that expression first, then choose the tense that matches it.

- **Simple past** goes with a finished time: "in 1928, last year, ago, in the nineteenth century, when + past event".
- **Present perfect** ("has/have + past participle") connects the past to now: "since 1970, for decades, so far, recently, over the past fifty years".
- **Past perfect** ("had + past participle") shows an action completed **before** another past event: "by the time + past, before + past, when + past" (for an earlier action).
- **Future perfect** ("will have + past participle") goes with "by + future time": "by 2050".

Remember: **since** is followed by a starting point ("since 1990"), while **for** is followed by a length of time ("for decades").

## Examples

- In 1928, Alexander Fleming **noticed** that a mold had killed the bacteria in a dish.
- Since the invention of the telescope, astronomers **have discovered** thousands of new stars.
- By the time Columbus reached the Americas, Indigenous peoples **had lived** there for thousands of years.

## Strategy

- Underline every time word or phrase in the sentence.
- Decide whether the action is finished, continuing to now, earlier than another past action, or completed by a future time.
- Check that every verb in the sentence fits the same time frame.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Present perfect with a finished past time**: "In 1928, Fleming has noticed" ... should be "noticed".
- **Simple past with "since"**: "Since 1970, temperatures rose" ... should be "have risen".
- **"Since" with a length of time**: "since decades" should be "for decades".
- **Simple past after "by the time" + past**: use the past perfect for the earlier action.
- **Present tense inside a past time frame**: "When the settlers arrived, forests are covering the island" should be "covered".
- **"By + future date"**: requires "will have" + past participle.
