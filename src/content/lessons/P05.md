# Adjective clauses

An adjective clause (also called a relative clause) describes a noun. It begins with a relative pronoun or adverb and contains its own subject and verb.

- **who** for people as subjects; **whom** for people as objects
- **which** for things; **that** for people or things in restrictive clauses
- **whose** + noun for possession (people or things)
- **where / in which** for places; **when / in which** for times

The relative pronoun **replaces** a noun in the clause, so never add another pronoun: "the architect who **he** designed" is wrong.

If the clause after the pronoun is already complete (it has a subject, a verb, and any needed object), you need a word like "where, when, whose", or **preposition + which** ("in which, by which, at which").

A clause set off by **commas** is nonrestrictive and cannot begin with "that".

## Examples

- Marie Curie, **who** won two Nobel Prizes, was born in Warsaw.
- Ernest Hemingway, **whose** novels are known for their simple style, won the Nobel Prize in 1954.
- The Amazon is a region **in which** thousands of plant species have not yet been identified.

## Strategy

- Find the noun the clause describes: a person or a thing?
- Decide what the pronoun does in its clause: subject, object, or possessive.
- Check whether the clause is complete; if it is, use "where/when/whose" or a preposition + "which".

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Extra pronoun**: "the architect who he designed" should be "who designed".
- **"which its" instead of "whose"**: "the islands, which its wildlife" should be "whose wildlife".
- **"that" after a comma**: "Sequoia trees, that can live" should be "which can live".
- **"whom" as a subject**: "The Inuit, whom have lived" should be "who have lived".
- **"who/whom" for things** or **"which" for people**.
- **"where" after a preposition**: "the rate at where" should be "at which".
- **Adjective clause in place of the main verb**: "A seismograph, which measures earthquakes," still needs a main verb.
