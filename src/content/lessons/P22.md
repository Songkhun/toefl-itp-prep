# Redundancy and wrong word

This pattern covers two kinds of errors: **redundancy** (saying the same thing twice) and **wrong word** (using a word that looks or sounds similar to the correct one but has a different meaning or grammar).

**1. Redundancy**: In formal writing, do not repeat an idea that a word already contains.
- "return back" → **return** ("return" already means "go back")
- "ascend upward" / "descend downward" → **ascend**, **descend**
- "discuss about the problem" → **discuss** the problem ("discuss" takes a direct object)
- "merge together", "cooperate together" → **merge**, **cooperate**

**2. Do or make?**
- **make**: a discovery, a decision, a mistake, progress, an effort, a contribution, a profit
- **do**: research, work, harm, damage, an experiment, homework, business

**3. Rise or raise? Lie or lay?**

| Verb | Object? | Forms | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| rise | no | rise, rose, risen | Warm air **rises**. |
| raise | yes | raise, raised, raised | Banks **raise** interest rates. |
| lie (be flat) | no | lie, lay, lain, lying | Crocodiles are **lying** in the sun. |
| lay (put down) | yes | lay, laid, laid, laying | Birds **lay** eggs. |

Note that "lay" is also the past tense of "lie": "Yesterday the cat **lay** in the sun."

**4. Like or alike?**
- **like** is a preposition and needs an object: Bats fly **like** birds.
- **alike** is not followed by an object. It usually comes at the end of a clause, after the verb: The twins look **alike**.

**5. Affect or effect?** **Affect** is usually a verb (pollution **affects** health). **Effect** is usually a noun (a strong **effect** on tides).

## Examples

- Scientists **made** an important discovery in 1953.
- Most bacteria **do** no harm to humans.
- Female sea turtles **lay** their eggs in the sand.

## Strategy

- If two underlined words seem to say the same thing, one may be redundant. Ask whether the meaning changes if you remove one.
- For rise/raise and lie/lay, look for an **object** after the verb. An object means "raise" or "lay."
- Memorize the most common **make** and **do** expressions.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Redundant word**: "returning back," "ascend upward," "discuss about."
- **Rise with an object**: "rise interest rates" should be "raise interest rates."
- **Raise with no object**: "Warm air raises" should be "rises."
- **Lay with no object**: "crocodiles laying motionless" should be "lying."
- **Make/do confusion**: "make harm" should be "do harm"; "did a discovery" should be "made a discovery."
- **Alike with an object**: "fly alike birds" should be "fly like birds."
