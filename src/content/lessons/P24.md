# Causatives

A **causative** verb shows that one person or thing causes another to do something, or arranges for something to be done. Each causative verb is followed by a specific verb form.

**1. Make + object + base form** (force or cause)
- Lords **made** peasants **work** on their land.
- Strong winds can **make** trees **sway**.

**2. Let + object + base form** (allow)
- The parks do not **let** visitors **feed** the animals.
- The floods **let** farmers **grow** crops in rich soil.

**3. Have + person + base form** (ask or arrange for someone to do something)
- The scientist **had** her assistants **record** the temperature.

**4. Get + person + to-infinitive** (persuade)
- Advertising **got** many Americans **to buy** products on credit.

**5. Have/get + thing + past participle** (arrange for something to be done by someone else)
- The museum **had** the manuscript **examined** by experts.
- Wealthy merchants **had** their portraits **painted**.
- She **got** her car **repaired**.

**Active or passive meaning?** Ask whether the object **does** the action or **receives** it. Assistants record the temperature (they do it) → base form. Portraits are painted (they receive it) → past participle.

**Note**: "Help" can be followed by either the base form or the to-infinitive ("help farmers grow" and "help farmers to grow" are both correct), so TOEFL does not test that choice. Also, in the passive, "make" takes "to": "The peasants **were made to work**."

## Examples

- Many national parks do not **let** visitors **feed** the wild animals.
- City officials **had** engineers **inspect** the old bridge.
- Augustus **had** many public buildings **rebuilt** in marble.

## Strategy

- Find the causative verb (make, let, have, get), then look at the verb form after its object.
- Make, let, have + person → base form (no "to").
- Get + person → "to" + verb.
- Have/get + thing that receives the action → past participle.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **"To" after make, let, or have** in the active voice: "let farmers to grow," "made trees to sway," "had engineers to inspect."
- **Base form instead of past participle**: "had their portraits paint" should be "painted"; "had their books copy" should be "copied."
- **Missing "to" after get + person**: "got many Americans buy" should be "to buy."
- **-s or past forms after the object**: "had her assistants records" should be "record."
