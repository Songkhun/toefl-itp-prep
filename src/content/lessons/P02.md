# Subject–verb agreement

A singular subject takes a singular verb ("the study reveals"), and a plural subject takes a plural verb ("the studies reveal"). The challenge on the TOEFL is **finding the real subject**, because the test places other nouns between the subject and the verb.

Find the head noun of the subject and ignore:

- prepositional phrases: "The migration **patterns** of the monarch butterfly **have** ..."
- adjective clauses: "The **policies** that the government introduced **were** ..."
- phrases like "along with, as well as, together with, in addition to": "Mathematics, along with physics, **is** ..."

Some subjects are always singular: "each, every, one of, either, neither, the number of", and gerund or noun-clause subjects ("Studying fossils **is** ..."). "A number of" is plural. Some nouns ending in -s are singular: "mathematics, physics, economics, news".

With "either ... or" and "neither ... nor", the verb agrees with the **closer** subject. After "There is/There are", the verb agrees with the noun that follows.

## Examples

- **Each** of the planets **orbits** the Sun in an elliptical path.
- **The number** of languages spoken worldwide **is** about seven thousand.
- Neither the director nor **the actors were** aware of the film's future success.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **The nearest noun is not the subject**: "The study of ancient climates reveal" ... should be "reveals".
- **"One of" + plural noun**: "One of the most famous paintings are" ... should be "is".
- **"Along with" / "as well as"** do not create a plural subject.
- **"The number of" vs. "a number of"**: singular vs. plural.
- **Agreement inside clauses**: in "problems that planners was unable to solve", the subject of the clause is "planners", so use "were".
- **Inverted sentences**: in "Not until recently have the effects been studied", the subject comes after the auxiliary, so check it carefully.
- **"There is/There are"**: look at the noun after the verb.
