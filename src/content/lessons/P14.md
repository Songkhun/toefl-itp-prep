# Gerunds and infinitives

A **gerund** is a verb + -ing used as a noun ("**flying**"). An **infinitive** is "to" + base verb ("**to fly**"). Some words require one form; using the other is an error.

**1. Verbs followed by a gerund**: avoid, enjoy, finish, consider, deny, suggest, postpone, admit, risk, mind, practice, appreciate.
- Bats **avoid flying** into obstacles.

**2. Verbs followed by an infinitive**: decide, agree, refuse, hope, plan, manage, fail, want, need, promise, expect, tend, seem, afford. (Note: "need + -ing" can have a passive meaning: "The roof needs repairing" = "needs to be repaired".)
- The expedition **managed to reach** the Pacific coast.

**3. After every preposition, use a gerund**: in, on, of, for, by, about, without, instead of, before, after.
- The Wright brothers succeeded **in making** the first powered flight.
- Lindbergh became famous **for making** the first solo nonstop flight across the Atlantic.

Be careful with "to" as a **preposition**: "be used to **working**", "look forward to **seeing**", "be devoted to **studying**".

**4. Infinitive of purpose** ("in order to"): "Lightning rods are installed **to protect** buildings."

**5. It + be + adjective + infinitive**: "It is important **to take** antibiotics as prescribed."

**6. Spend time / have difficulty + -ing**: "They **spent** years **experimenting**"; "Early aviators **had difficulty building**..."

Some verbs, such as begin, start, continue, like, and prefer, can take either form, so TOEFL rarely tests them.

## Examples

- Instead of **building** its own nest, the cuckoo lays its eggs in other birds' nests.
- Engineers **considered digging** a tunnel under the English Channel.
- Biologists have long **wanted to explain** how sea turtles find their home beaches.

## Strategy

- Look at the word just before the verb: a preposition always needs -ing.
- Memorize the most common gerund verbs and infinitive verbs.
- After "to", ask: is it part of an infinitive or a preposition?

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Base form after a preposition**: "famous for make" should be "famous for making".
- **Infinitive after a gerund verb**: "considered to dig" should be "considered digging".
- **Gerund after an infinitive verb**: "wanted explaining" should be "wanted to explain".
- **"To" + -ing in an infinitive**: "It is important to taking" should be "to take".
- **"For" + base verb for purpose**: "for protect" should be "to protect".
