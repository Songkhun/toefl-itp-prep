# Passive voice

In an **active** sentence, the subject does the action: "Farmers produce vanilla." In a **passive** sentence, the subject receives the action: "Vanilla **is produced** by farmers."

The passive is formed with **be + past participle**. The form of "be" shows the tense:

- "is/are produced" (present), "was/were built" (past)
- "has/have been studied" (present perfect)
- "must be confirmed, had to be copied" (modals and infinitives)

To decide between active and passive, ask: **Does the subject perform the action, or does something happen to it?** A phrase beginning with "by" that names the doer is a strong signal for the passive. Also check whether the verb has an object: an active transitive verb needs one, so if no object follows, the passive may be required.

## Examples

- The Great Wall of China **was built** over many centuries by successive dynasties.
- Most of the world's vanilla **is produced** in Madagascar.
- The Dead Sea Scrolls **have been studied** by scholars for more than seventy years.

## Strategy

- Identify the subject and ask whether it can logically do the action.
- If not, look for "be + past participle".
- Check that "be" agrees with the subject and matches the tense.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Missing "be"**: "Thousands of miles of track laid by workers" should be "were laid".
- **Base form instead of past participle**: "was dedicate" should be "was dedicated".
- **-ing instead of past participle**: "documents that were writing" should be "were written"; "have been studying" should be "have been studied".
- **Active form with a "by" phrase**: "The wall built by the dynasties" has no correct main verb.
- **Passive after modals**: "books had to copy by hand" should be "had to be copied".
- **Intransitive verbs** (such as "occur, happen, arise, exist") **never** take the passive: "was occurred" is always wrong.
