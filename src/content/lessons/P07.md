# Adverb clauses and connectors

An adverb clause tells **why, when, how much, or in spite of what** something happens. It begins with a conjunction and has its own subject and verb: "**Although** the platypus **is** a mammal, it lays eggs."

The most important skill is telling **conjunctions** from **prepositions** that have similar meanings:

- **Conjunction + subject + verb**: although, even though, because, since, while, when, as
- **Preposition + noun phrase**: despite, in spite of, because of, due to, during

Result clauses use two special patterns:

- **so + adjective/adverb + that**: "so loud that", "so quickly that"
- **such + (a/an) + adjective + noun + that**: "such a high salt content that", "such deep lakes that"

A sentence with an adverb clause still needs a complete main clause. The adverb clause alone is not a sentence.

## Examples

- **Although** Antarctica is covered by ice, it is classified as a desert.
- Many cacti survive droughts **because of** their ability to store water.
- The eruption of Krakatoa was **so** loud **that** it was heard thousands of kilometers away.

## Strategy

- Look at what follows the blank or the underlined connector.
- If a subject and a verb follow, you need a conjunction.
- If only a noun phrase follows, you need a preposition.
- For result clauses, check whether a noun comes after the adjective: noun → "such"; no noun → "so".

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Preposition before a clause**: "Despite Antarctica is covered by ice" should be "Although".
- **Conjunction before a noun phrase**: "because their ability" should be "because of their ability".
- **"During" + clause**: "during the revolution was transforming Britain" should be "while".
- **"so" before a noun**: "so deep lake that" should be "such a deep lake that".
- **"such" before an adjective alone**: "such dry that" should be "so dry that".
- **"In spite" without "of"**, or "despite of" (always wrong).
- **Two connectors for one idea**: "Although ..., but ..." uses one connector too many.
