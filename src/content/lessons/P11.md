# Comparatives and superlatives

**Comparatives** compare two things; **superlatives** compare three or more.

- One-syllable adjectives (and two-syllable adjectives ending in -y): **-er / the -est**: "harder, the longest, costlier".
- Most longer adjectives: **more / the most**: "more populous, the most productive".
- Irregular forms: "good → better → the best", "bad → worse → the worst", "far → farther → the farthest".

Key patterns:

- **Comparative + than**: "Diamond is much harder **than** graphite."
- **The + superlative**, often with "of", "in", or "one of the + plural noun": "one of **the most populous** metropolitan areas".
- **as + adjective + as**: "four times **as fast** in water **as** in air."
- **The + comparative ..., the + comparative**: "**The higher** a climber goes, **the thinner** the air becomes."
- With only **two** things, use the comparative: "Of the two moons of Mars, Phobos is **the larger**."

Never use **more** and **-er** together, or **most** and **-est** together. These double comparatives are always wrong.

## Examples

- Of all the bones in the human body, the femur is **the longest**.
- Saturn is much **larger than** Earth.
- **The more** carbon dioxide the atmosphere contains, **the more** heat it traps.

## Strategy

- Count the things compared: two → comparative; three or more → superlative.
- If you see "than", the word before it must be comparative.
- If you see "as ... as", the adjective between them stays in its simple form.
- Check the number of syllables to choose "-er" or "more".

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Double comparatives**: "more larger", "more greater", "more costlier".
- **Wrong form for short adjectives**: "the most high" should be "the highest".
- **Superlative with only two things**: "of the two moons, the largest" should be "the larger".
- **Missing "the"** in "the more ..., the more ..." or before a superlative.
- **Mixed patterns**: "as fast than" or "faster as".
- **"than" with a superlative**: "the hardest than graphite".
