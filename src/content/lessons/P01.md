# Subject and verb: missing subject or verb

Every English sentence needs **at least one subject and one main (finite) verb**. On the TOEFL, many Structure questions simply remove the subject, the verb, or both, and the wrong options look almost right.

A main verb shows tense: "is, was, carries, discovered, has grown". These forms are **not** main verbs by themselves: an -ing form ("carrying"), a to-infinitive ("to carry"), or a verb inside a clause ("which carried", "that carried").

A subject must be a noun, a pronoun, a gerund, an infinitive, or a noun clause. It cannot be repeated: if the sentence already has a subject, do not add "it", "they", or "he" before the verb (a "double subject").

When a sentence has two clauses, **each clause needs its own subject and verb**, and the clauses must be joined by a connector such as "although, because, when", or "and".

## Examples

- **The Erie Canal linked** the Great Lakes with the Atlantic Ocean. (subject + past-tense verb)
- Although coral reefs are small, **they support** a quarter of all marine species. (each clause has a subject and a verb)
- **The Rosetta Stone**, discovered in 1799, **allowed** scholars to read hieroglyphs. ("discovered in 1799" is extra information; "allowed" is the main verb)

## Strategy

- Find the main subject and main verb of the sentence.
- Cross out phrases between commas and prepositional phrases; they usually do not contain the main subject.
- Count clauses and connectors: the number of clauses should be one more than the number of connectors.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **-ing or to-infinitive used as the main verb**: "The canal linking" ... has no verb.
- **Double subject**: "The Mississippi River it drains" ... should be "The Mississippi River drains" ...
- **Adjective clause instead of a main verb**: "The canal, which linked the lakes," ... still needs a main verb.
- **Missing subject after a dependent clause**: "Although reefs are small, support many species" needs "they".
- **Extra verb in the subject**: "Many organisms live in the ocean produce light" has two main verbs; use "living".
- **"There" and "It" openers** that add an unnecessary second subject.
