# Noun clauses

A noun clause works like a noun: it can be the **subject** of a sentence, the **object** of a verb, or the **object of a preposition**. It begins with "that, whether, if", or a question word ("what, who, why, how, when, where, which").

Three key rules:

- **Statement word order**: inside a noun clause, the subject comes before the verb. "Archaeologists do not know how **the islanders moved** the statues" (not "how did the islanders move").
- **Choosing the connector**: use "that" for a complete statement; use "what" when the clause is missing a subject or object ("what affects spending"); use "whether" for yes/no ideas.
- **A noun-clause subject is singular**: "That the universe is expanding **was** shown in 1929."

As a subject or after a preposition, use **whether**, not "if". After "the fact", use **that**, not "which".

## Examples

- **Why the dinosaurs became extinct** is still debated by scientists. (subject)
- Economists study **what affects consumer spending** during a recession. (object)
- The question of **whether life exists on other planets** has fascinated scientists. (object of a preposition)

## Strategy

- Find where the clause sits: subject, object of a verb, or after a preposition.
- Check the word order: subject + verb, never auxiliary + subject.
- Check whether the clause is complete ("that, whether") or missing a part ("what, who").

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Question word order**: "how old is the fossil" should be "how old the fossil is".
- **"did/do" inside the clause**: "how do honeybees communicate" should be "how honeybees communicate".
- **"If" as a subject**: "If Neanderthals used language is debated" should begin with "Whether".
- **"what" vs. "that"**: "that" introduces a complete clause; "what" fills a missing subject or object.
- **"the fact which"** should be "the fact that".
- **Plural verb after a noun-clause subject**: use a singular verb.
- **"Because" instead of a noun clause** in the subject position.
