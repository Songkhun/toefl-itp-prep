# Appositives

An **appositive** is a noun phrase that renames or explains another noun right next to it. It contains **no main verb** of its own.

- Ada Lovelace, **a British mathematician**, wrote what is often regarded as one of the first computer programs.

An appositive can appear in three places:

- **After the noun**, set off by commas: "Penguins, **a group of flightless birds**, live..."
- **At the start of the sentence**, followed by a comma and the noun it renames: "**The longest river in Africa**, the Nile flows north..."
- **At the end of the sentence**, after a comma: "...triggered by earthquakes, **sudden movements of the Earth's crust**."

A short appositive that identifies a person can come right before the name with no commas: "**The Mexican painter** Frida Kahlo..."

An appositive can be followed by its own adjective clause: "Gregor Mendel, **a monk who studied pea plants**, ..."

Remember that the appositive is extra information. The main verb agrees with the **real subject**, not with the noun inside the appositive.

## Examples

- Tenochtitlan, **the capital of the Aztec Empire**, was built on an island in Lake Texcoco.
- **A region of the North Atlantic Ocean**, the Sargasso Sea has no land boundaries.
- Johann Sebastian Bach, **a German composer of the Baroque period**, wrote more than a thousand works.

## Strategy

- If the blank is between commas next to a noun, try a noun phrase with no verb.
- If an opening phrase is followed by a comma and then a noun, the phrase is probably an appositive.
- After you find the appositive, look for the main verb and check its agreement with the main subject.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Verb inside the appositive**: "Ada Lovelace, was a British mathematician, wrote..." creates two verbs.
- **Subject + verb inside the appositive**: "Tenochtitlan, it was the capital..." is a run-on sentence.
- **Relative pronoun with no verb**: "Bach, which a German composer..." should be "a German composer".
- **Extra pronoun after the appositive**: "The cello, a large string instrument, it produces..." is a double subject.
- **Agreement with the wrong noun**: "Penguins, a group of flightless birds, lives..." should be "live".
- **Missing main verb**: an appositive is not a verb, so the sentence still needs one.
