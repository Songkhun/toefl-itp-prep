# Conditionals and wishes

A **conditional** sentence has an **if-clause** (the condition) and a **main clause** (the result). The verb forms depend on whether the condition is real or unreal.

**1. Facts and general truths (zero conditional)**: present + present.
- If pure water **is heated** to 100 degrees Celsius at sea level, it **boils**.

**2. Real or possible future (first conditional)**: present + will/can/may + base verb.
- If the drought **continues**, many crops **will fail**.

**3. Unreal present or future (second conditional)**: past + would/could/might + base verb. In formal writing, "be" becomes **were** for all subjects ("If the Moon **were** closer..."); "were to" describes an imagined future event.
- If the Earth **had** no atmosphere, the sky **would appear** black.
- If bees **were to disappear**, many plants **would have** difficulty being pollinated.

**4. Unreal past (third conditional)**: had + past participle + would/could/might **have** + past participle.
- If the canal **had not been built**, ships **would have had** to sail around South America.

**5. Unless** means "if not": "Seeds will not germinate **unless** they receive moisture."

**6. Wish**: one tense "back" from the real time.
- Present wish → past: "Many farmers wish the rains **came** more regularly."
- Past wish → past perfect: "Many adults wish they **had learned** a second language."

**Do not use "will" or "would" to show the time of the condition in the if-clause** ("If the Earth will rotate…" → "rotated"; "If it would rain…" → "rained"). (Polite forms such as "if you would like" are an exception and are rarely tested.)

## Examples

- If the Earth **rotated** faster, days **would be** much shorter.
- If the Titanic **had carried** enough lifeboats, many more passengers **might have survived**.
- Many explorers later wished that they **had had** more accurate maps.

## Strategy

- Read the if-clause first: present, past, or past perfect?
- Match the main clause: will / would + base verb / would have + past participle.
- After "wish", move the tense one step back.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **"Will" in an unreal sentence**: "If the canal had not been built, ships will have had to..." should be "would have had to".
- **"Will" or "would" in the if-clause**: "If the Earth will rotate faster" should be "rotated".
- **"had" after a modal**: "might had survived" should be "might have survived".
- **Present tense after "wish"**: "they wish they have learned" should be "had learned".
- **"Unless" confused with prepositions**: "without" and "in case of" need a noun phrase, not a clause.
