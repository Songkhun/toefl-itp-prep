# Adjectives versus adverbs

**Adjectives** describe nouns and pronouns. **Adverbs** describe verbs, adjectives, other adverbs, and whole clauses. Many adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective: rapid → rapid**ly**, extreme → extreme**ly**, easy → eas**ily**.

**1. Use an adjective**:
- **Before a noun**: a **wide** range of sounds, a **remarkable** achievement.
- **After a linking verb** (be, seem, appear, become, look, sound, feel, smell, taste, remain, stay): Gold feels **heavy**. The landscape appears **lifeless**. The violins still sound **beautiful**.

**2. Use an adverb**:
- **To describe an action verb**: The population increased **dramatically**. Owls fly **silently**. Temperatures drop **sharply** after sunset.
- **Before an adjective**: **extremely** hot, **surprisingly** cold.
- **Before another adverb**: **almost** perfectly, so **rarely**.
- **Before an adjective that comes before a noun**: an **extraordinarily** long time (the adverb describes "long," not "time").

**3. Linking or action?** Some verbs can be both. "Look," "feel," "sound," "taste," and "smell" are linking verbs when they describe the subject: "The wings **look** blurry." They are action verbs when they describe an action: "She **looked** carefully at the map."

**4. Words that are both adjectives and adverbs**: fast, hard, late, early, daily, high, low. "Cheetahs run **fast**" is correct. "Hardly" and "lately" have different meanings from "hard" and "late." Words ending in -ly such as "friendly," "lovely," and "costly" are adjectives, not adverbs.

## Examples

- Sea otters **skillfully** use rocks as tools. (adverb describing "use")
- The surface of Venus is **extremely** hot. (adverb describing the adjective "hot")
- Honey can remain **edible** for an **extraordinarily** long time. (adjective after "remain"; adverb before "long")

## Strategy

- Find the word that the underlined word describes. Is it a noun (adjective) or a verb, adjective, or adverb (adverb)?
- If the verb is a linking verb, the word after it describes the subject and must be an adjective.
- When two describing words come before a noun, check whether the first one describes the noun or the second word.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Adjective after an action verb**: "increased dramatic" should be "dramatically"; "float easy" should be "easily".
- **Adverb after a linking verb**: "still sound beautifully" should be "beautiful"; "appears lifelessly" should be "lifeless".
- **Adjective before an adjective**: "an extraordinary long time" should be "an extraordinarily long time"; "extreme hot" should be "extremely hot".
- **Adjective at the start of a verb phrase**: "Sea otters skillful use rocks" should be "skillfully use".
