# Word form

Many English words belong to a **word family**: a noun, a verb, an adjective, and an adverb built on the same root. In Written Expression, a common error is a word from the right family but in the **wrong form** for its position in the sentence.

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| invention | invent | inventive | inventively |
| danger | endanger | dangerous | dangerously |
| erosion | erode | erosive | — |
| accuracy | — | accurate | accurately |

**1. Nouns** are needed:
- After **articles and possessives**: **the** erosion, **their** color, **a** threat.
- Between **"the ... of"**: the **maintenance** of healthy skin, the **warming** of the planet.
- After **adjectives**: bacterial **infections**, great **accuracy**.
- As **subjects** and **objects**, including after prepositions: with **accuracy**, without **emissions**.

**2. Verbs** are needed after a subject, after modals (can **damage**), and after "to" (to **propel**).

**3. Adjectives** describe nouns and follow linking verbs:
- **skillful** swimmers, an **important** discovery, a **complex** dance.
- Coral reefs are extremely **sensitive**.

**4. Adverbs** describe verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs: navigated **successfully**, **extremely** sensitive. (See P20 for adjective-versus-adverb choices.)

**Common endings** can help you identify the form:
- Nouns: -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance/-ence, -ure
- Verbs: -ize, -ify, -en, -ate
- Adjectives: -ous, -ful, -ive, -al, -able/-ible, -ic, -ant/-ent
- Adverbs: -ly

## Examples

- The **erosion** of topsoil is a serious problem for farmers. (noun after "The")
- Marie Curie won two Nobel Prizes for her **important** discoveries. (adjective before a noun)
- Many bats hunt with great **accuracy**. (noun after "great" and the preposition "with")

## Strategy

- Look at the word **before** and **after** the underlined part. An article or adjective before it usually means a noun; a noun after it usually means an adjective.
- Ask what job the word does in the sentence: subject, object, describer of a noun, or describer of a verb.
- Check the ending. A verb or adjective in a noun position ("the erode of") is a quick error to spot.

## TOEFL traps to watch for

- **Verb in a noun position**: "The invent of the telescope" should be "The invention".
- **Noun in an adjective position**: "skill swimmers" should be "skillful swimmers"; "her importance discoveries" should be "important".
- **Adjective in a noun position**: "pose a serious dangerous" should be "danger"; "with great accurate" should be "accuracy".
- **Verb after an adjective**: "harmful emit" should be "harmful emissions".
- **Look-alike words**: "hard" (adjective), "hardly" (adverb meaning "almost not"), and "hardness" (noun) have different meanings.
